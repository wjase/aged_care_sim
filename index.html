  Assumptions
  
    
    
  • The daily fees (basic, hotelling, higher Every Day Living Fee, NCCC and the DAP) are charged for 365 days a year, with no indexation. Home value grows at the rate above until sold.
    • 
    
  • Each year runs in this order: house sale, then the RAD is topped up to the maximum from the proceeds (start of year), investment income on the resulting balance, defined benefit income added, retention and daily fees drawn, remaining costs paid by selling investments, then the RAD is topped up back to the maximum from investments.
    • 
    
  • From the sale year the refundable RAD is kept at the room max RAD at the end of every year. The sale-year top-up comes from the house sale money (not taxed, topped up from investments if short). Later annual top-ups are paid by selling investments, grossed up for tax. If investments run short, fees are paid first and the RAD top-up takes what is left.
    • 
    
  • By default fees are debited from the refundable RAD balance (after retention), which reduces the refund but not the RAD outstanding. Once that balance is used up, the rest is paid from the investments pool. You can instead debit only the DAP from the RAD, or everything from investments.
    • 
    
  • Any RAD top-up and any fees not covered by the RAD are funded from the investments pool. Only the house sale money used to top the RAD up to the maximum in the sale year is untaxed. Any other money drawn from the investments pool (including leftover house sale money) is grossed up for tax: tax = amount sold × taxable share × marginal rate, charged in the year of sale.
    • 
    
  • The defined benefit income is taxable income. It is taxed at the marginal rate each year and the after-tax amount is paid into the investments pool. Investment income is not taxed in this model.
    • 
    
  • Accommodation: the RAD outstanding is the room max RAD less the refundable RAD balance, so retention and fees drawn from the RAD increase it. The daily accommodation payment (DAP) is charged at the MPIR rate on the outstanding amount at the start of the year, after any top-up. Topping the RAD up to the maximum reduces the DAP to zero.
    • 
    
  • Retention: a % of the current refundable RAD balance is deducted each year for up to the maximum years. It is a non-cash cost taken from the refundable balance.
    • 
    
  • NCCC stops once either the time cap or the value cap is reached. It is not means-tested here. The caps, the MPIR and the fee amounts are editable and should be checked against current legislation and Services Australia figures.
    • 
    
  • Monthly expenses are charged for 12 months a year with no indexation and are paid from the investments pool (they cannot be debited from the RAD). Housing expenses run until the house is sold and are zero from the sale year onward. Non-housing expenses continue for the whole period.
    • 
    
  • Selling the home attracts no tax (main residence). Selling costs are deducted from the proceeds and count as an outgoing cost. Stamp duty is paid by the buyer, so it is not modelled.
    • 
    
  • The simulation stops in the first year any balance goes negative (for example the investments cannot cover the fees and expenses). That year is the last row, the balance that went negative is highlighted in red, and the report names it.
    • 
    
  • This is a planning illustration, not financial advice.
